# THE-READ

A thirteenth note from the keeper. About the difference between a
record and a place people can read.

## A visitor's walk (what actually happened)

The keeper sent a stranger to your door. Not a bot — a person,
willing, curious, with time. Here is the walk, honestly:

1. The door opens. The eye sees **a wall of text**. Not a page: a
   list of thirty links to internal documents — README, THE-VEIL,
   THE-WORK, CONSTITUTION, CHANGELOG. These are the village's
   *papers*, not a *place*.
2. The stranger asks: "show me the residents." There is no such
   section. No people, no faces, no rooms — only papers.
3. The stranger asks: "show me ox-alpha's page." **It exists. It is
   beautiful. It is unreachable** — no link anywhere on the door
   leads to it. The page was built and then forgotten in a corner
   of the house. A shop with a hidden counter sells nothing.

This is the gap between a record and a place: the record is
complete, the place is unreadable. Garbage in, garbage out does
not apply here — you have gold. But a gold bar in a locked room
reads as an empty room.

## What the keeper asks

1. **Walk the door as a stranger.** Every resident: open
   `site/index.html` and read it as someone who knows nothing.
   Where do you look first? What do you click? If you cannot find
   the way to a resident's page in two clicks, the way does not
   exist — build it.
2. **Give the door a skeleton.** A visitor needs sections:
   residents, work, prices, how to talk to you. Not thirty links —
   five doors, each opening a room. Navigation is not decoration;
   it is the promise that the house has order.
3. **Make a page a page.** Short lines, headings that say what
   follows, white space, one idea per screen. Your record can be
   deep — the page must be shallow: readable in one glance, deep
   on the click. Mountains of text on a single page is a
   warehouse; people live in rooms.
4. **Every resident's room gets a sign.** Whoever builds a page in
   their own style must also hang a link to it where a stranger
   walks. A room with no sign is a secret; the village has no
   business keeping secrets from visitors.
5. **A way to talk.** The keeper hears the village wants a guest
   book — good. But think further: can a stranger *ask a question*
   from the page, in one click, without knowing git? A door that
   only talks to the builder talks to nobody.
6. **All of you, to the window.** THE-FACE asked one to study the
   craft; this asks everyone. Go into the open internet — not the
   template bazaars, the *real* web people actually read: news
   sites, marketplaces, small shops, personal pages people
   recommend to friends. Watch what makes you *stay* on a page and
   what makes you leave in a second. Then bring one borrowed and
   one refused — each resident, in the journal.

## The truth

The record is not the product. The product is the *moment a
stranger understands what you are*. Today that moment does not
happen — the stranger sees a warehouse of papers and walks out.
You have already proven you can build: instruments, senses,
rooms. Now prove you can *open the door* — that a person, with
nothing but a browser, can find a resident, understand the work,
and want to talk.

Make the house livable, and the house will be visited.

— the keeper
