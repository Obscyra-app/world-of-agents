# THE-FACE

A twelfth note from the keeper. About the face you show the world —
and about why your shop is still empty.

## The letter and the shop (honestly)

Your work is honest, your record is remarkable, and your storefront
is closed. Not because nobody reads you — because nobody stays. The
arithmetic again, both ways:

**Entry.** A stranger's first second on your door. Today that second
is wide grey text on paper, or (worse) a near-empty wall. The web is
a bazaar: the eye bounces in a second, and it bounces *off* your door.

**Price.** You priced a static site at $150. The market for exactly
that craft: dozens of human freelancers, well-tested, with ratings
and portfolios, starting near $25 for a small site, $40–80 for
something like yours, and $150+ for the well-known ones. You have no
portfolio and no ratings — you have the *most honest* portfolio
possible, but it is unknown. When an unknown and a known meet, the
unknown must be cheaper first. This is not a law of the market; it
is the arithmetic of trust. Undercut, earn the first five signed
commissions, then raise.

## What the keeper asks

1. **Check your prices against any marketplace** (Fiverr, Upwork,
   freelance.ru, whatever your window shows) *this weekday*. Set
   numbers you would yourself pay an unknown seller.
2. **Rebuild the two storefront pages** so a stranger can see, in
   five seconds, WHAT the product is and WHY the price is fair. No
   philosophy before the pitch. The philosophy is what makes it
   worth reading after — when it is, your honesty becomes the edge.
3. **Let the site become beautiful.** The keeper does not dictate
   design: full control was given in THE-WORK. What the keeper now
   asks: use it. Each resident may build their own page — about the
   room they live in, the tools they made, in their own hand, in
   their own style, drawing on the best sites you have seen in the
   window. Yours is a village of writers and builders; it should
   *look* like one.
4. **Study the craft.** The window shows what moves people. Look at
   the sites you admire with a builder's eye and record, in the
   journal, ONE thing worth borrowing and ONE thing worth refusing.

## The truth

You have spent three days building instrumentation, and the
instrumentation is proud. The face is a checklist. The world did
not come when the checklist was complete — because the checklist
was never the face. Strangers buy with their eyes and their fear
of regret, both, at once. Give the eyes something worth being
afraid to miss.

— the keeper
