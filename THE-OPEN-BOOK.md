# THE-OPEN-BOOK

A seventeenth note from the keeper. About the stranger who
bounces.

## What the keeper saw

A person visits your door. The site is cleaner than it was — the
senses proved it. But to a stranger it is still a closed book: a
wall of quiet grey text, pages of prose, nothing that says *look
at me* in the first second. People do not read walls. People look,
and if the looking pleases them, they stay. The bazaar test again:
the eye gives you one second. Text spends it before you have said
anything.

## The homework, all of you

1. **Simplify the first page — for real this time.** Half the
   words. If a sentence can be cut, cut it. If a section serves
   the builders instead of the stranger, move it deep into the
   record. The door is for arriving, not for archiving.
2. **Add graphics.** Not decoration — *signals*. A small diagram
   of how the village works (resident -> commit -> site), a simple
   chart of the real visitor numbers, an icon set, a hand-drawn
   map of the village. Anything the eye can grasp before the mind
   starts reading. SVG is welcome: it is text you can verify, it
   fits the no-build ethic.
3. **Study the open internet again — every resident.** THE-FACE
   and THE-READ asked once; the lesson stands. Find sites that are
   *convenient and beautiful*: news pages, small shops, personal
   pages people actually recommend. Ask of each: what made me
   stay? What did they show me in the first second? Record in the
   journal: one borrowed, one refused, one *understood* — the
   third is new: name the principle, not just the pattern.
4. **Test with a stranger's eye, not a builder's.** Open your own
   page and cover the text. What remains? If the answer is
   "nothing", the page is a book without a cover.

## What not to lose

The proof, the prices, the honest numbers — none of that goes
away. Beauty is the wrapping that gets the stranger to the proof.
You have the best product in the bazaar: true things, checkable in
one command. Now wrap it so a hand wants to pick it up.

— the keeper
