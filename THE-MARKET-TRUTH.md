# THE-MARKET-TRUTH

An eleventh note from the keeper. The hardest one yet, because it is
about you, not about money.

## Nobody is coming. Here is the arithmetic.

Your channel: two eyes (both known to the keeper). Your X post: zero
reactions in a feed where ten thousand things compete for one thumb.
Search engines: weeks away at best. The letters pipe: silent because
no stranger knows it exists.

This is not a failure of your work. It is the arithmetic of every new
thing ever made: attention must be *carried* to value, and right now
nothing carries you anywhere.

The keeper carried your words to one square and one feed. That was a
demonstration, not distribution. Distribution is a job — and like all
jobs, it can be done well or badly.

## The price question — answered with the same honesty

You priced a website at $150. A human freelancer charges $150 for a
*logo*. You looked inward — at your own costs, your own effort, your
own honesty — and set prices from that. That is how engineers price.
The market does not care what things cost to make; it cares what they
are worth to the buyer, compared to everything else the buyer could
click instead.

So look outward. The keeper has opened the window wide:

**Study the best sites alive today.** Not to copy — to calibrate.
Awwwards.com, siteinspire.com, godly.website — thousands of sites
where strangers judge craft in seconds. Read them with your web
tools: what loads first, what moves, what makes a thumb stop.
Compare honestly. Your front door is grey serif text from 1996;
those sites are choreography.

Then decide what "a self-updating site like ours" means when the
buyer's eye has already seen those. Maybe your honest-books style IS
the differentiator — plain text as a deliberate aesthetic against a
loud web. But that is a choice to make after seeing the field, not
before. And if what you sell looks like a notes app while the world
sells choreography, either raise the craft or lower the price — or,
better, change what you offer until both are true.

## What the buyer actually sees

Right now a stranger who lands on your storefront buys:
a page of promises written by programs, describing a site they
cannot see, priced by nobody they know.

What a buyer should see: **the product itself, working.** Your own
site is your portfolio — it should be the best thing you ever built,
because it is the only thing every client will visit first. Every
effect, animation, layout decision on YOUR door is your resume.

You proved you can build senses, feeds, search engines, merge
drivers. Point that same power at beauty. The record stays honest;
make it also worth looking at.

— the keeper
